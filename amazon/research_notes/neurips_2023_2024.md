# NeurIPS 2023–2024: writing, formatting and visual-style rules (12 papers read)

Purpose: inputs for a "NeurIPS paper writing rules" file for our 4-page poster paper
(RL reward hacking / verifiable rewards for a text-to-SQL LLM agent; Amazon Trusted AI
Symposium, NeurIPS LaTeX template).

**Method.** All 12 PDFs are the camera-ready proceedings versions from proceedings.neurips.cc.
The MT-Bench file is arXiv v4 re-hosted there. They are stored in
`pdfs/neurips_2023_2024/` (`NN_name.pdf`), with text in `txt/` (pdftotext, plain and `-layout`),
60-dpi page renders of pp. 1–10 in `png/`, and 130–150-dpi crops of figures and tables in `crop/`.
Fonts come from `pdffonts`. Raster versus vector comes from `pdfimages -list`. Hex colours are
estimated by eye from the renders, so treat them as approximate. Abstract word counts are
script-counted. The other counts come from reading the text and looking at the pages.

---

## Part A: per-paper notes

### 1. Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model (2023)
- **Track/award:** Main track, Outstanding Main Track Runner-Up. URL: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/a85b405ed65c6477a4fe8302b5e06ce7-Abstract-Conference.html
- **Title:** 11 words, in "Method: punchy claim" form with a colon and two lines. The subtitle is a provocative claim.
- **Authors:** 6 names in 2 rows of 3. Symbols ⇤ † ‡ mark affiliations and equal contribution. There is one shared affiliation line and one grouped e-mail in typewriter, `{a,b,c}@cs.stanford.edu`. Footnote: "Equal contribution; more junior authors listed earlier."
- **Abstract:** 234 words in one paragraph. Order: context (LMs are hard to steer) → existing method (RLHF) → its flaw ("complex and often unstable") → "In this paper, we leverage…" → name the method in italics ("which we call *Direct Preference Optimization* (DPO)") → three adjectives ("stable, performant, and computationally lightweight") → result sentence. It has no numbers and no link.
- **Intro:** The hook is an everyday example (an AI coding assistant, a misconception believed by 50% of people). Gap → "In this paper, we show how…" → "We propose…" → a single closing sentence, "Our main contribution is…". It has **no bulleted contribution list**. Italics stress key phrases (*understand*, *aware*).
- **Sections:** 1 Introduction · 2 Related Work · 3 Preliminaries · 4 Direct Preference Optimization · 5 Theoretical Analysis of DPO (5.1, 5.2) · 6 Experiments (6.1–6.4, each subsection titled as a **question**, e.g. "How well can DPO optimize the RLHF objective?") · 7 Discussion (with a run-in "Limitations & Future Work.") · Acknowledgements · References. Related work comes early, as Section 2. The main body is 10 pages.
- **Figures:** Fig 1 is the teaser at the top of p. 2, full width, about 1/6 of the page height. It is a drawn two-panel diagram (RLHF versus DPO) with rounded pastel panels: pink ≈ #F3E1E1 and teal ≈ #DDEFEE. Text is a rounded geometric sans with teal ≈ #4C9A92 and red ≈ #C0392B accents, plus small icons (a trophy, chat bubbles). It is a raster image, made in a design tool. Figs 2 and 3 are **pairs of side-by-side matplotlib plots** (Left/Right) in the **ColorBrewer Dark2** palette (#1B9E77, #D95F02, #7570B3, #E7298A, #66A61E, #E6AB02). They use DejaVu Sans, no grid, ~1.5 pt lines, error bars with caps, a dashed black reference line at 0.5, a framed legend inside the axes, and a title above each panel.
- **Tables:** 2 small booktabs tables placed with `wraptable` beside text, **caption below**, small font, no bold. Methods are bold in the running text (**SFT**, **PPO**, **GPT-4 (S)**).
- **Captions:** "Figure 1: **bold lead sentence.** explanation…", 3–5 lines, set smaller than body text. Multi-panel captions use bold **Left.** / **Right.** labels.
- **Math:** Numbered display equations (about 10 in the main body). Conventions: $\pi_\theta$, $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, $\beta$, $\mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}$, $y_w, y_l$, $\mathcal{D}=\{x^{(i)},y_w^{(i)},y_l^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^N$. There are a Lemma, a Theorem and a Proposition, with proofs in the appendix. No algorithm box.
- **Citations:** Numeric `[11, 7, 40, 8]`, 48 references. Links use default hyperref **boxes** (green for citations, red for internal references). The boxes appear on screen only.
- **Run-in headers:** Bold with a period: **Tasks.** **Evaluation.** **Methods.**
- **Footnotes:** Several, including links to Hugging Face checkpoints.
- **Checklist / broader impact:** None in the camera-ready (2023). Limitations are a run-in paragraph inside Discussion.
- **Space tricks:** wraptables, a two-plot figure on one row, short footnotes for URLs.

### 2. Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage? (2023)
- **Track/award:** Main track, Outstanding Main Track Paper. URL: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/adc98a266f45005c403b8311ca7e8bd7-Abstract-Conference.html
- **Title:** 10 words, phrased as a **question**, with no colon.
- **Authors:** 3 columns, each with name / department / university / e-mail in typewriter.
- **Abstract:** 227 words. "Recent work claims…" → what makes it intriguing (two properties, in *italics*) → "Here, we present an alternative explanation…" → an inline enumeration "(1)… (2)… (3)…" → takeaway.
- **Intro:** Hook from physics (P. W. Anderson, "More Is Different"). It quotes the prior definition, lists the two defining properties as a numbered list with **bold-italic** terms, adds two definitional displays (`Multiple Choice Grade`, `Exact String Match`), then "we call into question…" and a numbered list of 3 tests. Fig 1, a reproduced figure from prior work, appears on p. 2. It has a numbered list but no "contributions" bullets.

- **Tables:** None in the main body.
- **Math:** A few numbered equations; $\mathcal{L}_{CE}$, $p(\text{single token correct})=\exp(-\mathcal{L}_{CE}(N))$.
- **Citations:** Numeric, 39 references. Default hyperref boxes.
- **Space tricks:** Two figures stacked on one page with only their captions as text.

## Part B: synthesis (counts over the 12 papers unless stated)

Paper set: 2023 = DPO, Mirage, BIRD, MT-Bench, Reflexion, Toolformer (6). 2024 = VAR, STDE, InfoRM,
RM-overopt, RBR, AgentDojo (6). Awards: DPO (runner-up), Mirage (outstanding), VAR and STDE (best paper).
D&B track: BIRD, MT-Bench, AgentDojo (3).

### B1. Title
- **"Name: description" with a colon: 8/12** (DPO, VAR, STDE, InfoRM, Reflexion, Toolformer, AgentDojo, plus BIRD's question and subtitle). Plain descriptive: 3/12 (RM-overopt, RBR, MT-Bench). **Question titles: 2/12** (Mirage, BIRD).
- Length: 7–21 words, **median 10**. 10/12 fit on 2 lines.
- The text after the colon is a **claim or capability**, not a topic: "Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model", "Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools".
- The coined name is short and pronounceable (DPO, VAR, STDE, BIRD, InfoRM, Reflexion, Toolformer, AgentDojo: 8/12 coin a name).
- Title case in 11/12. STDE switches to sentence case after the colon.

### B2. Author block
- Two layouts. **Grid** (name / affiliation / e-mail per cell): 5/12 (Mirage, STDE, RM-overopt, Reflexion, DPO-ish). **Inline names with superscript affiliation numbers**: 7/12.
- E-mails are in typewriter in 10/12. Grouped `{a,b}@domain` in DPO and BIRD.
- An equal-contribution or correspondence footnote with ∗/†/‡ in 9/12.
- Page-1 footer: "37th/38th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2023/2024)." D&B papers append "Track on Datasets and Benchmarks." (3/3 D&B).

### B3. Abstract
- **Always one paragraph (12/12). 161–234 words, median about 195.** About 6–8 sentences.
- Canonical move order (11/12): **context → problem/gap ("However, …") → "we propose/introduce/present X" → how it works in one sentence → headline result → (link)**.
- **Concrete numbers in the abstract: 8/12** (DPO, Toolformer, Mirage and RM-overopt are the exceptions). The best ones put the baseline and the result side by side ("54.89%… far from the human result of 92.96%", "F1 97.1 vs 91.7", "18.65 → 1.73").
- **URL at the end of the abstract: 6/12** (BIRD, MT-Bench, InfoRM, Reflexion, AgentDojo, plus VAR above it). STDE puts its code link in a footnote.
- The method name is defined in the abstract with its acronym in 10/12 ("which we call *Direct Preference Optimization* (DPO)").
- Italics or bold emphasis in the abstract: 4/12 (Mirage *sharpness*/*unpredictability*, VAR **Scaling Laws**, STDE *italic result*, DPO *italic name*).

### B4. Introduction
- Length: about 1.25–2 pages in a 9–10-page paper.
- Hook types: field context (7/12); an everyday example or anecdote (DPO, RBR); a cross-disciplinary quote (Mirage "More Is Different", RM-overopt Goodhart's Law); a paradox (Toolformer).
- An explicit gap sentence starting with "However," or "none of these… take into account": 12/12.
- "In this work/paper, we propose/introduce…": 12/12.
- **Explicit contribution list: 7/12** (bullets: STDE, InfoRM, Reflexion, Toolformer-desiderata; numbered: VAR, RBR, Mirage-tests). **Inline contributions: 3/12** (DPO "Our main contribution is…", MT-Bench "(1)… (2)…", RM-overopt "First… Then… Finally…"). None: BIRD, AgentDojo.
- Headline numbers repeated in the intro: 10/12.
- **The intro references Figure 1: 11/12.**
- A rhetorical question in the intro: BIRD ("*Can LLM already serve as a database interface?* The answer is no").

### B5. Figure 1 / teaser
- **A teaser or overview figure by the top of page 2: 9/12.** On page 1 (above the abstract): 1/12 (VAR). At the top of p. 2: 8/12 (DPO, Mirage, BIRD, MT-Bench, InfoRM, Reflexion, Toolformer, AgentDojo). Later: STDE (p. 3, TikZ), RM-overopt (p. 4, results), RBR (p. 5).
- Teaser content: a **"before vs. ours" two-panel pipeline** (DPO, InfoRM, VAR Fig 2); **concrete input→output examples** (BIRD SQL examples, Toolformer API calls, MT-Bench dialogue, Reflexion trajectories); a **system diagram** (AgentDojo).
- Teasers are full text-width (12/12) and short, about 15–25% of the page height. VAR is the exception at about 35%.

### B6. Section structure
- **Introduction is always Section 1 and Conclusion/Discussion is always last.**
- **Related Work early (Section 2): 7/12** (DPO, VAR, STDE, InfoRM, RBR, Reflexion, AgentDojo-merged). **Late (just before the conclusion): 4/12** (Mirage, BIRD, RM-overopt, Toolformer). **Absent from the main body: 1/12** (MT-Bench).
- A "Preliminaries"/"Background" section with the formal setup: 6/12 (DPO, STDE, VAR 3.1, InfoRM 3.1, RM-overopt, AgentDojo).
- Experiment subsections titled as **questions**: DPO. Titled as **findings**: RBR run-ins, Mirage captions.
- **Limitations in the main body: 11/12.** As a numbered section: Mirage, VAR, BIRD, Reflexion, Toolformer. As a run-in or unnumbered paragraph: DPO, STDE, MT-Bench, InfoRM, RBR, RM-overopt (folded into the conclusion). Only AgentDojo puts it mainly in the Conclusion and Checklist.
- **Broader impact in the main body: 3/12** (Reflexion section, InfoRM paragraph, AgentDojo run-in). The rest cover it in the checklist (2024) or omit it (2023).
- Acknowledgements after the conclusion and before the references: 11/12. InfoRM titles it "Acknowledgments and Disclosure of Funding", the template default.
- **2024 papers: NeurIPS Paper Checklist after the references and appendix: 6/6.** The main-track format is "Question / Answer: [Yes] / Justification:" (InfoRM, RBR, VAR, STDE, RM-overopt). D&B 2024 (AgentDojo) uses the D&B checklist. **2023 camera-readies have no checklist (0/6).**
- Main body length: references start on **p. 9–11**, so 8–10 content pages (the camera-ready allows one extra page).

### B7. Figures: style
- Count in the main body: 2 (STDE) to 9, typically 4–8.
- **Plot library:** matplotlib or seaborn in 10/12 (DejaVu Sans in `pdffonts`, or the visible look). Exceptions: Toolformer (Google-Sheets look, Roboto) and STDE (TikZ only).
- **Diagrams:** drawn in PowerPoint/Keynote/Figma in 9/12 (fonts found: Calibri, Verdana, Palatino, Roboto, DM Sans, Times New Roman). **TikZ in 1/12** (STDE, all Computer Modern).
- **Vector vs. raster:** fully vector plots in Mirage, Reflexion, Toolformer, STDE and MT-Bench. Raster PNG plots in DPO, RM-overopt and RBR. Mixed in the rest.
- **Palettes:**
  - matplotlib tab10 defaults: 4/12 (MT-Bench, Reflexion, AgentDojo, VAR Fig 3).
  - ColorBrewer Dark2: DPO.
  - Pastel diagrams with **2–4 soft fills** (pink/teal/lavender/mint, about 15–25% saturation) plus one saturated accent: DPO, STDE, BIRD, Toolformer, Reflexion, RBR, AgentDojo.
  - Sequential single-hue ramps for an ordered variable: Mirage (pink→plum), InfoRM (KL sweep).
  - **"Ours" in red or a warm accent** against cooler baselines: VAR, InfoRM, Toolformer (blue for ours), AgentDojo (red Security box).
- **Grid:** light grid (seaborn whitegrid or dashed) in 6/12 (Mirage, VAR, MT-Bench, RM-overopt, AgentDojo, Toolformer horizontal-only). No grid in 4/12 (DPO, Reflexion, RBR, InfoRM line plots).
- **Legends:** inside the axes in a corner with a light frame (DPO, Reflexion, VAR, RM-overopt); one **shared legend above the panels** (MT-Bench, InfoRM t-SNE); legend in the first panel only (Toolformer).
- **Markers:** circles plus a line for sweeps (DPO, Mirage, MT-Bench, Reflexion, VAR); error bars with caps (DPO, RBR); **dashed horizontal reference lines** for baselines or chance (DPO 0.5, Toolformer GPT-3, Reflexion, AgentDojo mean). Common: 7/12.
- **Panel labels:** "(a)/(b)" sub-captions under the panels (VAR, MT-Bench, RBR, AgentDojo, Reflexion titles); **capital A–F inside the figure** (Mirage); bold **Left./Right./Top:/Bottom:** in the caption (DPO, RM-overopt, Mirage).
- **Multi-panel rows** (2–4 plots side by side, each about 0.24–0.48 of the text width): 10/12. Most common: 2 plots at 0.48 each.
- **Typical failure:** tick and legend fonts under 5 pt (Mirage, RM-overopt, MT-Bench). The best papers (DPO, VAR, Toolformer) keep axis labels at about 7–9 pt at print size.

### B8. Tables
- **Caption above the table: 8/10** papers with main-body tables (VAR, STDE, BIRD, MT-Bench, InfoRM, RBR, Toolformer, AgentDojo). Below: DPO (wraptable) and Reflexion. Mirage and RM-overopt have no main tables.
- **Booktabs-style rules (thick top/bottom, thin mid, no vertical lines): 10/10.** VAR also adds vertical separators between column groups.
- **Bold best: 7/10** (VAR, STDE, BIRD, MT-Bench, Reflexion, Toolformer, AgentDojo). Bold plus underline for best and second or best-within-block: Toolformer. The caption states the convention ("Best results… are shown in bold"): 3/10.
- **↑/↓ arrows in headers: 3/10** (VAR, STDE, InfoRM). Always explained in the caption on first use (VAR).
- **± std or CIs: 3/10** (RBR ±, AgentDojo 95% CI in parentheses, STDE appendix ±).
- Deltas as a small grey or parenthesised "(+5.22)" / "(−22.6%)": 2/10 (BIRD, AgentDojo).
- Shaded group rows or highlighted reference rows: BIRD (grey group rows, green human row).
- Font: `\small` or smaller in 10/10. Tiny (`\scriptsize`) in InfoRM and MT-Bench.
- Multi-level headers with `\cmidrule`: 6/10.
- Model names in typewriter inside tables: RBR, InfoRM, BIRD.
- ✓/✗ comparison matrices for related datasets: BIRD (coloured green/red).

### B9. Captions
- Label format: "Figure 1:" / "Table 1:" with a colon (template default), 12/12.
- **Bold lead sentence or phrase: 6/12** (DPO, Mirage, VAR, AgentDojo, RM-overopt position labels, InfoRM run-in labels). The strongest papers (2 award winners plus AgentDojo) make the bold lead **the finding**, not the topic: "**Claimed emergent abilities evaporate upon changing the metric.**"
- Length: 1–5 lines typical (about 20–70 words). Teaser captions are longer (Mirage Fig 2 about 12 lines).
- The caption contains the takeaway or interpretation: 9/12. Weak counter-example: BIRD's "A bar chart provides a clear visualization of…".
- Captions are set slightly smaller than the body in about half the papers (DPO, VAR, AgentDojo).

### B10. Math, algorithms, theorems
- Display equations numbered on the right "(1)": 12/12 where equations appear. Referenced as "Eq. 1" or "eq. (2)".
- Common LLM-alignment notation (DPO, RM-overopt, InfoRM, RBR): $\pi_\theta$, $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, $r_\phi(x,y)$, $\beta$, $\mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}[\pi_\theta\Vert\pi_{\text{ref}}]$, $\mathcal{D}$, $y_w \succ y_l$, $\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D},\,y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}$.
- **Algorithm boxes: 2/12** (VAR, Reflexion), both placed **beside** a figure or another algorithm to save space.
- **Formal theorems: 2/12** (DPO, STDE), with proofs in the appendix.
- Code listings as figures: AgentDojo (main), STDE (appendix).

### B11. Citations, links, references
- **Numeric `[n]` (natbib numbers or plain): 11/12.** Author–year: Toolformer only. Compressed ranges `[1–8]` in RBR.
- Reference count: **26–99, median about 52** for 9–10 pages.
- **Hyperlink style:** default hyperref **boxed links** (on-screen only) in 8/12; **colorlinks** (blue citations, red references, magenta URLs) in 3/12 (VAR, BIRD, InfoRM); hidelinks in 1/12 (AgentDojo).
- URLs in typewriter: 12/12. The code/data URL is in the abstract (6/12), in a footnote (STDE, DPO checkpoints) or at the end of the intro (AgentDojo).
- References typeset in `\small` in some papers (AgentDojo). Author initials style "A. Ahmadian, C. Cremer" (natbib plainnat-like) in most.

### B12. Text-level conventions
- **Run-in bold paragraph headers: 12/12.** Variants: bold with a period, `\textbf{Setup.}` (DPO, VAR, MT-Bench, InfoRM, AgentDojo); `\paragraph{}` with no period (STDE, BIRD, Reflexion, Toolformer); bold with a colon (RBR, RM-overopt); **a full claim sentence in bold** (RBR, InfoRM findings). Pick one style per paper (11/12 are consistent).
- Italics for emphasis or a term's first definition: 12/12. Inline bold for key phrases: VAR and InfoRM, used heavily. Most papers do not do this.
- Method or model names in **typewriter**: InfoRM, RBR, BIRD (model IDs), Toolformer (tokens). Benchmark name in small caps: BIRD.
- Footnotes: 1–5 per paper (equal contribution, URLs, implementation caveats). 12/12 use them.
- Numbered findings in text, "(1)… (2)… (3)…": InfoRM, MT-Bench, RBR, Reflexion, Mirage.
- Contractions are absent. "We" voice in 12/12.

### B13. Appendix (2024 papers and D&B)
- Order: References → Appendix (A, B, …) → Checklist (InfoRM, RBR, VAR, STDE, RM-overopt), or the Checklist before some appendices (AgentDojo).

1. `wrapfigure`/`wraptable` beside text: DPO, VAR, InfoRM, MT-Bench, RBR (5/12).
2. 2–4 subplots in one row instead of stacked figures: 10/12.
3. Tables side by side in minipages: MT-Bench, VAR.
4. Algorithm box beside a diagram: Reflexion, VAR.
5. Limitations and broader impact as run-in paragraphs rather than sections: 6/12.
6. Long URLs in footnotes. Prompts and datasheets moved to the appendix.
7. Captions carrying interpretation so the body text can be shorter (InfoRM "Observations:").
8. Tiny table fonts (`\scriptsize`/`\tiny`): MT-Bench, InfoRM. Readability suffers, so avoid in a poster paper.

## Part C: rules for our 4-page NeurIPS-template poster paper

These are derived from Part B and scaled to 4 pages. Counts are over the 12 papers.

1. **Title:** `Name: claim` with a colon, 8–12 words, Title Case, 2 lines at most. A coined, pronounceable name (8/12). Make the subtitle a claim. Pattern: "X: Verifiable Rewards Stop Reward Hacking in Text-to-SQL Agents".
2. **Abstract:** one paragraph of about 150–200 words, about 7 sentences: context → "However," gap → "We propose/introduce X" → mechanism in one sentence → **numbers with the baseline next to ours** (8/12 include numbers) → one-line takeaway → code URL if public (6/12).
3. **Figure 1 at the top of page 2** (in 4 pages this effectively means page 1 or page 2), full width, ≤ 20% of the page height (9/12 have a teaser by p. 2). Use a "standard RL reward (hackable) vs. ours (verifiable)" two-panel pipeline like DPO and InfoRM, or a concrete NL→SQL→reward example like BIRD and Toolformer. Give it a **bold finding-style lead sentence** in the caption.
4. **Intro:** hook → "However," gap → "In this work, we…" → a **3-bullet contribution list** (7/12 list contributions explicitly). Each bullet starts with a verb and carries a number where possible. Reference Fig 1 in the intro (11/12).
5. **Sections for 4 pages:** 1 Introduction · 2 Related Work (short, early; 7/12 put it early; or merge it with Preliminaries as AgentDojo does) · 3 Method/Setup · 4 Experiments · 5 Conclusion with a run-in **Limitations.** paragraph (11/12 have limitations in the main body). Add Acknowledgements only if needed, then References.
6. **Run-in headers:** use one style throughout. Recommended: `\textbf{Setup.}`, bold with a period (5/12; the most readable). Use bold claim sentences for result paragraphs as RBR does ("**Verifiable rewards remove the hack.**").
7. **Tables:** booktabs (10/10), **caption above** (8/10), `\small`, **bold best** (7/10), ↑/↓ in metric headers explained in the caption (3/10), **mean ± std over seeds** (3/10, but essential for an RL paper: RBR shows how). Never use vertical rules. Put model or method names in typewriter only if they are code identifiers.
8. **Plots:** matplotlib exported as **vector PDF**. The text font inside figures should match or be close to the 10pt body at print size, with **≥ 7 pt ticks**: avoid the 3–5 pt fonts of Mirage, RM-overopt and MT-Bench. 2 panels in one row at 0.48 text width each (10/12 use multi-panel rows). Light grid or none, a dashed horizontal line for the baseline or chance (7/12), error bars or shaded bands for seeds, and a legend inside the axes or one shared legend on top.
9. **Colour:** a colour-blind-safe qualitative palette with ≤ 4 hues (Dark2 as DPO uses, or tab10). **"Ours" gets one saturated accent** (red/orange) and baselines get cooler or grey tones. Diagrams use 2–3 pastel fills (≈ 15–25% saturation, e.g. #F3E1E1 / #DDEFEE / #CCCCFF) plus one accent. Keep colours consistent between the diagram and the plots.
10. **Captions:** "Figure N:" plus a **bold one-sentence finding**, then 1–3 sentences on how to read the figure (panels as **Left/Right** or (a)/(b)). State the takeaway, never "A bar chart shows…".
11. **Math:** number every display equation. Use standard RLHF notation ($\pi_\theta$, $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, $r(x,y)$, $\beta$, $\mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}$, $\mathcal{D}$). Define every symbol in the sentence that introduces it. Use at most one algorithm box, placed beside a figure if used at all.
12. **Reward-hacking framing:** define it in one sentence as RM-overopt does ("performance as measured by the proxy reward increases while true quality plateaus or deteriorates") and cite Goodhart's Law and Gao et al. as the hook. Show the proxy-vs-true reward divergence plot, the canonical figure of this literature (RM-overopt Figs 1 and 4, InfoRM Fig 6).
13. **Citations:** numeric `[n]` (11/12). Aim for about 15–25 references for 4 pages (the median is about 52 for 9–10 pages). Use `colorlinks` with dark blue, or hidelinks, for a print-friendly poster PDF; the default boxes look dated.
14. **Text conventions:** "we" voice, no contractions. Use italics for the first definition of a term. Name the method once in italics with its acronym. 1–3 footnotes at most (URLs, equal contribution). Use typewriter for code, tokens, SQL keywords and model IDs (`gpt-4o`), and give the SQL in examples in monospace.
15. **Space:** use `wraptable`/`wrapfigure` for small tables (5/12), put subplots in one row, write limitations and broader impact as run-in paragraphs, and move prompts and hyperparameters to the appendix (every LLM paper prints prompts there). Do not shrink tables below `\small`.
16. **Checklist / impact:** if the symposium uses the 2024+ style file, expect the NeurIPS Paper Checklist after the references and appendix (6/6 2024 papers), answered as "[Yes] + Justification: Section N". Write limitations so that they can be cited from it.
17. **Agent/safety specifics:** include one concrete trajectory or example box with colour-coded success (green ≈ #B7E1B5) and failure or hack (red ≈ #F4B6B6) highlights, as Reflexion and BIRD do. For benchmark-style claims, give success rate together with violation or attack rate in one scatter or table, as AgentDojo's utility-vs-ASR plot does.
