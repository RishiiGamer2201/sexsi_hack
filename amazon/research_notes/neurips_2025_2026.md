# NeurIPS 2025–2026 papers: writing, formatting and visual-style notes

Purpose: input for the "NeurIPS paper writing rules" file. Target: a 4-page poster paper (NeurIPS LaTeX template) for the Amazon Trusted AI Symposium, on RL reward hacking and verifiable rewards for a text-to-SQL LLM agent.

Method (2026-11-21): 14 PDFs downloaded with curl into `pdfs/neurips_2025_2026/`. Text came from `pdftotext`, which I used to count abstract words, headings, captions, references and environments. I rendered pages 1–12 at 60 dpi (`pdftoppm`, in `pdfs/neurips_2025_2026/renders/`) and looked at the title page, the teaser page and 1–3 figure or table pages of every paper. I estimated figure colours by sampling the rendered pixels and binning them to 24 levels per channel, so hex values are approximate.

Sources and versions:
- NeurIPS 2025 best papers and runner-ups: camera-ready PDFs from papers.nips.cc (P02, P04) or the arXiv camera-ready (the footer reads "39th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2025)").
- NeurIPS 2026 (40th, December 2026): there are no proceedings yet. I used arXiv camera-readies whose footer reads "40th Conference … (NeurIPS 2026)". Two are preprint-mode builds of the NeurIPS style (P08, P13). I checked accept status and oral/spotlight on the OpenReview API, venue strings such as "NeurIPS 2026 oral".
- OpenReview PDF downloads are behind a Cloudflare challenge, so I could not use them.
- NeurIPS 2026 best-paper awards were not announced as of this date.
- The 2026 D&B track was renamed **"Evaluations and Datasets Track"**. Footer: "Track on Evaluations and Datasets".

## Template facts seen in every paper (NeurIPS style file)

- Title block: bold, centred, about 17 pt, between a **thick rule above and a thin rule below**. All 14 have it.
- "Abstract" is centred and bold. The abstract is a single indented paragraph (14/14).
- Body is Times 10 pt in one column (5.5 in text width). Section headings are bold, numbered and in sentence or title case. There are no section breaks between pages.
- Camera-ready footer on page 1 (13/14): "39th/40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2025/2026)." The D&B or E&D track name is appended for that track. P08 and P13 are built in "Preprint" mode.
- The arXiv margin stamp appears on arXiv copies only. It is not part of the template.
- Main text reached references at pages 10–13. Most papers sit at 9–10 content pages plus refs, so camera-ready gets +1 page. Then come references, appendix and the NeurIPS checklist. Six arXiv or proceedings copies keep the checklist (P01–P04, P11, P12). It now includes item **"16. Declaration of LLM usage"** (2025+).

### P01 Artificial Hivemind (2025 D&B, Best Paper)
- **Title.** 13 words, 2 lines, "Concept: descriptive subtitle (and Beyond)". A small emoji/logo sits before the title.
- **Authors.** 11 authors, compact, with ♠♢⋆ symbol affiliations. Under them sit a code link (GitHub icon) and a dataset link (HF icon) with monospace URLs.
- **Abstract.** 275 words in one paragraph. It runs problem, gap, "To address this gap, we introduce INFINITY-CHAT", taxonomy, finding (bold **Artificial Hivemind**, italic "intra-model repetition" / "inter-model homogeneity"), then human-annotation data and a closing "first large-scale resource". Dataset name is in small caps.
- **Intro.** Hook on homogenisation of thought, then the gap (no scalable diversity evaluation), then "We introduce…". The key finding is bolded inline and contributions are given as inline (1)/(2). There is no bullet list.
- **Figures.**
  - Fig. 1 sits at the top of p2, full width: a PCA scatter of 25 models with a colour legend on the left and example-text panels on the right in pastel highlight boxes.
  - 11 figures in the main body: heatmaps (orange/red sequential, about #FC843C→#E42424, values printed in cells), bar charts, histograms, a word cloud and a taxonomy.
  - Bar palette: periwinkle **#849CCC**, tan **#E4CC9C** for the "All" bar, salmon **#FC846C**. Values are printed above bars and there is a light horizontal grid.
  - Panel labels are bold "(a)"/"(b)" at the top left. Fonts are sans-serif and small (smaller than the caption). Plots look like matplotlib.
- **Captions.** 28–69 words. They **bold the key finding mid-caption** (for example "two visually prominent regions") and sit below the figure.
- **Citations.** Numeric [n], about 95 references. Run-in bold headers are often whole claim sentences ("**Models show weaker alignment with human ratings for alternative responses of similar quality.**").
- **Sections.**
  - Order: 1 Intro, 2 dataset, 3 finding, 4 analysis, 5 Related Work, 6 Conclusion, then Acknowledgment.
  - Limitations, Future Directions and Broader Implications are moved to appendix A.1–A.3. The appendix also holds full prompts.
- **Space tricks.** Wrapped figures (Fig. 3 word cloud, Fig. 8) sit beside text.

- **Sections.** 1 Intro, 2 Related Work (early), 3 Dataset, 4 Evaluation Framework, 5 Experiments, 6 Conclusion. The appendix has a contents list: Use of LLMs, Broader Impact, Licensing, Limitation, Future Work, human-validation protocol.

## Synthesis: rules with counts (n = 14)

### A. Title, authors, abstract
1. **Titles fit on 2 lines** (13/14). Length is 4–16 words, median about 11.
2. **"Name/Concept: descriptive subtitle" is the dominant pattern** (9/14). Questions are rare (1/14, P04). Five use a short system or benchmark name before the colon (SQL-R1, BIRD-RL, M4Bench, MLS-Bench, LITMUS).
3. **Abstract is one paragraph** (14/14). Length is 161–310 words, median about 215. Since our 4-page paper needs a short one, aim for **150–200 words**.
4. **Abstract skeleton** (12/14): context, then gap or failure ("However…"), then "We introduce/propose X" (or "we study"), then mechanism in one sentence, then **concrete numbers** (11/14 give at least one number such as %, ×, places or sizes), then release or URL (6/14).
5. **Emphasis in the abstract**: 9/14 italicise or bold the key term or number once or twice. Never more than about 3 emphasised spans.
6. Authors with superscript numeric or symbol affiliations (11/14) instead of the default grid (3/14). Code, project or data links sit under the authors with icons in 4/14 (P01, P04, P12, P14), or as the last abstract sentence or a footnote otherwise.

### B. First two pages
7. **A teaser or overview figure is on page 1 or the top of page 2** in 12/14 (11 at the top of p2, 1 on p1 under the abstract: P10). Only P06 (p3) and P11 (p5) delay it.
   - For a 4-page paper this means **Figure 1 should be on page 1 or at the top of page 2**.
   - P10's layout (a short full-width results strip directly under the abstract) is the model when space is tight.
8. **Teaser type**: about half are **results-first** teasers (P01, P03, P05, P06, P10, P11), and half are drawn **method or environment overviews** (P02, P04, P07, P08, P09, P12, P13, P14). Topic-close papers on RL or agents use a **pipeline/environment illustration + one key plot** (P02, P04 combine both in one figure).
9. **Intro structure**: hook, then gap ("However/Yet"), then "In this work/paper, we…", then contributions.
   - **Explicit contribution list** in 8/14 (bullets with **bold lead-in claims** in 6 of those; numbered 1–3 in 2). Inline (i)/(ii) or (1)/(2) in another 4/14.
   - Contribution bullets often point to sections ("In Section 3 we…", P09) or carry labels (C1)–(C3) (P14).
10. **The research question is stated as a bold or italic sentence** in 3/14 (P04, P05, P07). This is useful for a reward-hacking question.

### C. Section structure
11. Canonical order (10/14): Introduction, then Preliminaries/Background (6/14 have a "Preliminaries" or problem-setup section with notation), then Method, then Experiments (Setup, Main results, Analysis/Ablation), then Related Work, then Conclusion.
12. **Related Work placement**:
    - **Late**, after experiments, in 9/14 (all four best papers or runner-ups except P03).
    - **Early (§2)** in 5/14: P03, P11, P12, P13, P14. Early placement is typical for benchmark or evaluation papers (3 of the 4 E&D/benchmark-style ones).
    - In a 4-page paper, compress it to one paragraph, either inside the intro or right before the conclusion.
13. **Limitations are in the main body** in 9/14, as a run-in "**Limitations.**" inside the conclusion or as "Conclusion and Limitations" / "Discussion and limitations". Only 2/14 push them entirely to the appendix (P01, P14).
14. Conclusion title variants: "Conclusion" (8), "Conclusion and Limitations" (2), "Conclusion and Discussion" (1), "Conclusion and Future Work" (1), "Discussion" (1).
15. Acknowledgments appear in 11/14 (camera-ready only). The NeurIPS checklist, with **16. Declaration of LLM usage**, is kept in 6/14 public PDFs. Broader-impact text is in the appendix or checklist, not in the main body.
16. Appendices are lettered (A, B, C…) with numbered subsections (A.1). **Full prompts** (judge prompts, system prompts, reward prompts) appear in 9/14. Case studies or trajectories appear in 6/14. P14 even starts the appendix with a contents list.

### D. Figures
17. **Main-body figure count**: 1–12, median 4. With 4 pages, **2–3 figures** is the realistic maximum (including the teaser).
18. **Width**: the teaser is full width (13/14). Later figures are often **half width with the caption beside them or two figures side by side** (wrapfig or minipage) in 6/14 (P01, P03, P04, P06, P08, P09). This is the main space-saving device.
19. **Plot fonts**: sans-serif (DejaVu or Helvetica, the matplotlib default) in 9/14. **Serif fonts matching the Times body** in 4/14 (P04, P09, P10, P13). One uses monospace (P07, and it looks off).
    - The cleanest-looking result plots (P04, P09, P10) are the serif ones.
    - Rule: **pick one font family for all figures. Serif (Times/STIX) matches NeurIPS best.**
20. **Figure text size**: 6/14 have figure text that is clearly **smaller than the caption and hard to read at 100%** (P05 legend, P06, P08, P11, P13 overview, P14 infographic).
    - Rule: **tick, legend and label text ≥ 7 pt after scaling (roughly ≥ 80% of caption size)**. Make the figure at its final printed width; do not shrink a large figure.
21. **Palettes** (estimated hex):
    - Muted blue/grey baselines with a warm colour for the proposed method: P10 (ours #B4543C versus #6C84B4/#CCB484/grey), P05 (#E45454 versus #5484B4), P01 (#849CCC versus #FC846C).
    - Sequential single-hue palette for ordered variables: P03 blues #9CCCE4 to #0C3C6C for depth.
    - Pairs: teal and salmon (#6AAF9A / #EE9A7A, P06).
    - Rainbow 15-colour categorical palettes appear only in P11, and look worst.
    - Rule: **≤ 5 hues, colour-blind safe, "ours" = one saturated warm colour, baselines = muted cool or grey, ordered variables = one sequential hue.** Also use different markers or hatching (P09, P13) so it survives grayscale.
22. **Legends**: one shared legend for multi-panel figures, above or below the panels (P03, P05, P10, P13). Some label lines directly in the plot instead (P09). Per-panel legend repetition is avoided.
23. **Panel labelling**: "Left/Middle/Right" named in the caption (bold) in 5/14 (P02, P04, P08, P10, P13). "(a)/(b)" sub-captions in 3/14 (P01, P09, and P10's circled A/B/C). Either is fine; be consistent.
24. **Uncertainty**: shaded std bands or error bars appear in plots of 5/14 (P03, P05, P09, P13, P14). Light horizontal grid lines appear in about half. Values are printed on bars in 4/14 (P01, P06, P11, P14).
25. **Diagrams** are drawn in vector tools (PowerPoint, Figma or draw.io style: rounded pastel panels, icons, emoji ✓/✗ badges) in 10/14. None look like TikZ.
    - Pastel fills (#E4E4FC, #CCE4E4, #FCFCCC, #FCE4E4) with one saturated accent are standard.
    - Red "Rejected"/"Reward Hack!" versus green "Accepted" tags are used for reward-related figures (P09, P10, P07). **This fits our topic directly.**

### E. Tables
26. **Caption above the table**: in every table I inspected, across all 14 papers. **Figure caption below**: 14/14.
27. **Booktabs** (top, mid and bottom rules, no vertical rules) in 11/14. A single vertical rule before an "Avg" column appears in P13. Shaded header or group rows appear in P06 and P14.
28. **Table text is smaller than the body** (\small or \footnotesize) in about 9/14. Most tables have 5–8 columns. The widest has 14 columns (P13), and needed \footnotesize.
29. **Best-result marking**: bold best in 8/14, **bold + underline second-best** in 2/14 (P08, P10). When used, this is **stated in the caption** ("Bold marks the maximum, underline the second-best.").
30. **"Ours" row(s)**: separated by a \midrule and/or bolded (P07, P08, P10). Gains shown as a coloured Δ column (green "+18.20", P08).
31. **± std or CI in main tables**: only 3/14 (P14 as small subscripts, P12 with 95% bootstrap CIs, P09 in text). Most report single numbers. Reviewers increasingly expect seeds or CIs for RL.
    - Rule: **report mean ± std over seeds (subscript ± in \scriptsize keeps columns narrow) or 95% CIs, and state n in the caption.**
32. **↑/↓ arrows in headers**: rare, 2–3/14 (P11 in cells, P08 in text for deltas). Units are given in the caption or header as "(%)" in 6/14 ("Execution accuracy (%)", "Success Rate (SR %)").
    - Rule: put the unit in the header, and add ↑/↓ only when metrics of both directions share a table (for example, reward-hack rate ↓ next to accuracy ↑, which is exactly our case).
33. Grouped rows with italic or multirow group labels (*Qwen3-4B*, *Vanilla*, "General-Purpose Models") appear in 6/14.

### F. Captions
34. **Figure captions are long and self-contained**: typically 30–70 words (range 4–82). They say *what to conclude*, not just what is plotted (about 10/14).
35. **Bold first sentence = takeaway** in 2/14 (P03, P05, both highly visible papers). **Bold key phrase inside the caption** in P01. **Bold panel names** in 4/14. For a poster paper, the bold-takeaway first sentence is the most reader-friendly option.
36. Table captions define every abbreviation and the aggregation ("arithmetic means over tasks", "greedy decoding", "averaged over N seeds"): P02, P08, P13, P14.

### G. Text style, math, citations
37. **Bold run-in paragraph headers** (`\paragraph{Setup.}`, `\textbf{Results.}`) appear in **13/14**, the most universal style device.
    - Common labels: Setup / Datasets / Models / Metrics / Results / Findings / Takeaway / Limitations.
    - Some papers make the run-in a **full claim sentence** (P01, P08 "(1) **Considerable Challenge…:**", P14 "**Finding 1:**").
38. **Findings as section or subsection titles** ("Models Learn to Evade Monitors", P09; "Distillation Expands the Reasoning Boundary", P04) appear in 4/14. This works well for analysis papers.
39. **Takeaway or recommendation callout boxes** appear in 3/14 (P02 framed box, P09 rounded grey, P13 pale blue italic). In a 4-page paper, use **at most one**.
40. Bold sentences inside paragraphs to highlight the headline result appear in 7/14. Italic is used for newly defined terms (*one-sided verifiability*, *reward hacking*) in 10/14.
41. Names: small caps for benchmark or condition names (P01, P06, P08, P12) and monospace for code, identifiers or method names (`HedgeTune`, `output.csv`, SQL keywords) in 6/14.
42. **Math notation** (RL/LLM papers):
    - Policy π_θ and reference π_ref; expectations E_{x∼D, y∼π_θ(·|x)}.
    - Rewards r(x,y) or r*; group size G for GRPO.
    - \mathcal for sets, events or datasets; definitions with :=.
    - Displayed equations are numbered on the right. The full GRPO objective is written out in P07 (and in P08/P10 or the appendix). Reward components are written as cases-equations (P07).
    - Formal Theorem/Definition environments appear only in theory-leaning P05 (1/14). Algorithm boxes in the main body appear only in P05 (1/14); others put them in the appendix (P08, P04).
43. **Citations**: numeric [n] in 11/14, author-year natbib in 3/14 (P03, P09, P12). Reference counts run about 20–122, median about 58. A 4-page poster paper should cite **15–25**.
44. **Link style**:
    - Default hyperref **coloured boxes** (green cite, red ref, cyan URL; on-screen only) in 8/14.
    - **Coloured link text** (blue P04/P13, green P07, grey-blue P03) in 4/14.
    - Black in 1/14.
    - Boxes look dated. Prefer `\hypersetup{colorlinks=true, citecolor=<dark blue>, linkcolor=<dark red or blue>, urlcolor=<dark magenta>}` with dark, print-safe colours.
45. Footnotes are used in all 14: equal contribution, corresponding author, code URL, terminology asides (P05, P11). Keep them to 1–2 in 4 pages.

### H. Space-saving tricks seen
46. Wrapfigure or side-by-side figure+caption (6/14). Two figures sharing a row with separate captions (P06, P09). Figure + table side by side (P07).
47. Inline enumerations (i)/(ii)/(iii) instead of list environments (6/14). Compact bullet lists with bold lead-ins.
48. Merged sections: "Conclusion and Limitations", "Background and Related Work", "Discussion and limitations".
49. Long details (prompts, hyperparameters, extra ablations, case-study trajectories) all go to the appendix with forward pointers ("see Appendix C.2") in 14/14.

### I. Small details worth copying
- State the decoding protocol in one run-in sentence (temperature, top-p, max tokens, k), as P04 and P07 do. The table caption repeats "greedy decoding".
- Define every metric once in a bulleted list with bold names, and give formulas for custom metrics (P14 ASR/EHR, P09 "Key Metrics" subsection). A reward-hacking-rate metric deserves the same treatment.
- Give a glossary table when there are many conditions (P12 Table 1).
- Use a coloured Δ column against the base model (P08). Green means improvement; it pairs well with ↑/↓.
- Name colours in the caption when the meaning is colour-coded ("Grey indicates…, Green…", P04), so the figure is not colour-only.
- Use real-incident hooks in the first paragraph for safety topics (P09: recent publicised reward-hacking cases). Text-to-SQL papers open with "NL2SQL enables intuitive interactions with databases…" (P07) or enterprise data apps (P08).
- Report statistical tests, CIs and multiple-comparison corrections for safety and evaluation claims (P09, P12, P14 κ for judge validation). Validate any LLM-judge with human agreement (P14 Table 3), which is directly relevant if we use an LLM judge to detect hacks.

## Top 15 rules for our 4-page NeurIPS-template poster paper

1. **Title.** 2 lines or fewer, "Name: claim-style subtitle" (9/14).
2. **Abstract.** One paragraph of 150–200 words: context, gap, "we…", mechanism, ≥ 2 concrete numbers, code link (12/14 follow this skeleton; 11/14 give numbers).
3. **Figure 1 on page 1 or at the top of page 2, full width** (12/14). It should combine our environment or pipeline sketch (SQL agent, verifier, where the hack enters) with one key result plot. Red "hack" and green "verified" tags are an established visual idiom (P07, P09, P10).
4. **Intro.** Hook, gap ("However…"), "In this work, we…", then a 3-item contribution list with **bold claim lead-ins** (8/14 have an explicit list).
5. **Section order.** Intro, Setup/Preliminaries (notation, reward definition), Method, Experiments, Related Work (one paragraph), Conclusion with a run-in **Limitations.** (9/14 keep limitations in the main body).
6. **Use bold run-in paragraph headers everywhere** (`\paragraph{Setup.}`, `Results.`, `Takeaway.`), as 13/14 do. Claim-sentence run-ins work well for findings.
7. **Captions.** Figures below, tables above (14/14). Captions should be self-contained, 25–60 words, and **open with a bold takeaway sentence** (P03, P05 style).
8. **Tables.** Booktabs with no vertical rules (11/14), \small, ≤ 7 columns. Bold best and underline second-best, *stated in the caption* (2/14 use the underline; 8/14 bold the best). Units in headers "(%)", and ↑/↓ when hack-rate ↓ and accuracy ↑ share a table.
9. **Uncertainty.** Report mean ± std over seeds (subscript ±) or 95% CIs and give n. Only 3/14 do, so doing it signals rigour for RL claims.
10. **Figure typography.** One font family across all figures; serif (Times/STIX) matches the body best (the 4/14 that do this look cleanest). Make figures at final width with text ≥ 7 pt. 6/14 fail this and it shows.
11. **Colour.** ≤ 5 colour-blind-safe hues. "Ours" is one saturated warm colour; baselines are muted cool or grey. Ordered variables (for example training step or penalty weight) get one sequential hue. Add markers or hatching for grayscale (P03, P09, P10, P13). Avoid rainbow palettes (P11 is the bad example).
12. **Multi-panel figures.** One shared legend; panels named "Left/Middle/Right" or (a)/(b), bold in the caption, used consistently.
13. **Space.** Save it with wrapfig or side-by-side minipages (6/14), inline (i)/(ii) lists, a merged "Conclusion and Limitations", and everything else (prompts, hyperparameters, extra ablations) in the appendix with forward pointers.
14. **Math.**
    - Write the GRPO/RLVR objective and the reward as numbered displayed equations, with π_θ, π_ref, G and r(x,y). Give the reward components as a cases-equation (P07).
    - Define the hack-rate metric formally (P14/P09 style).
    - No theorem environments unless we prove something (1/14).
15. **Citations and links.**
    - Numeric citations (11/14), 15–25 references.
    - `colorlinks` with dark, print-safe colours instead of default hyperref boxes (8/14 still show boxes, and they look dated).
    - Footnotes kept to ≤ 2.
    - Keep the NeurIPS checklist or LLM-usage declaration if the symposium template asks for it.
