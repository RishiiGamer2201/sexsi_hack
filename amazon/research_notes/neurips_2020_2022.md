# NeurIPS 2020–2022 papers: writing, formatting and visual-style notes

Purpose: input for the "NeurIPS paper writing rules" file. Target: a 4-page poster paper (Amazon Trusted AI Symposium, NeurIPS LaTeX template) on RL reward hacking and verifiable rewards for a text-to-SQL LLM agent.

Corpus: 13 camera-ready PDFs from the official proceedings (papers.nips.cc / datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc). 2020: 3, 2021: 4 (1 D&B), 2022: 6 (1 D&B).
Local copies: `pdfs/neurips_2020_2022/*.pdf`. Extracted text is in `txt/`, 60-dpi page renders in `png/`, 6-page contact sheets in `png/sheets/` (plus `palette.txt`), and 120-dpi crops in `png/hi/`.

Method: pdftotext (layout and raw) for text, pdffonts for figure fonts (DejaVuSans means default matplotlib), pdfimages for raster images, and `pdftotext -bbox-layout` for font sizes and text-block width. I viewed every main-body page as a rendered PNG, plus 120-dpi crops of key figures and tables. Hex colours come from pixel counts on the renders (quantised to 16 levels) and from looking at the crops, so treat them as ±1 shade.

Measured on all 13: the text block is **396 pt wide (5.5 in), x = 108–504 pt** on US Letter. Body line box is 8.91 pt high, i.e. 10 pt Times (NimbusRomNo9L). Caption first-line box is the same 8.91–9.03 pt in **12/13** papers (captions stay 10 pt). The exception is Precipice, at 8.12 pt (`\small` captions).

Note on abbreviations: "Fig." means figure. "run-in" means a bold `\paragraph{Title.}` heading followed by text on the same line.

### 2020-1. Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)

- **Tables:** 5 results tables (3.1–3.5) with horizontal rules only, best value in **bold**, and superscript letters a, b, c, d citing where each SOTA comes from. Captions sit **below** with a **bold first sentence** that states the finding ("GPT-3 significantly improves SOTA on LAMBADA…"). Table text is about 9 pt. No ±.
- **Citations:** alpha-style keys `[RWC+19]` in **blue** text (colorlinks). About 95+ references.

- **Space:** Broader Impacts sits outside the page limit (allowed in 2020).

## Part 2: Synthesis — rules with counts (n = 13 unless stated)

### A. Page geometry and fonts (template invariants)
1. Do not touch the geometry. In 13/13 papers the text block is 396 pt (5.5 in) wide starting at x = 108 pt, body is 10 pt Times, headings are bold, and the title sits between a thick top rule and a thin bottom rule.
2. Captions use body size: 12/13 keep 10 pt captions. Only Precipice used `\small`. Shrinking captions is allowed but rare.
3. The first-page footer is fixed text, bottom left:
   - 2020: "34th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2020), Vancouver, Canada."
   - 2021: "35th … (NeurIPS 2021)."
   - 2022: "36th … (NeurIPS 2022)."
   - D&B papers append "Track on Datasets and Benchmarks."
   - For the symposium, replace this line with the venue line the organisers ask for (or the `preprint` option); never leave a wrong year.
4. Camera-ready length: the 2021–2022 main text ends on p10 (references start on p10–11 in 9/10 papers). 2020 allowed 9 pp plus an unlimited Broader Impact section. References, acknowledgements and the checklist do not count.

### B. Title and author block
5. Title Case in 10/13; sentence case in 3/13 (Stiennon, InstructGPT, and LAION after the colon). Median length is 6 words (range 5–11). "Name: description" is used for method or dataset releases (3/13: DT, CodeRL, LAION). Claim or verb titles are common ("… Elicits Reasoning …", "Defining and Characterizing …", "Learning to …", "Training … to follow …").
6. Equal-contribution `*` footnote in 11/13. A single shared affiliation goes on one centred line (OpenAI, DeepMind, Google) rather than being repeated. Emails are grouped in braces `{a,b}@domain` (5/13). One paper put the code URL in the author block (CodeRL).

### C. Abstract
7. One paragraph in 12/13 (CoT used 2). Length is 100–278 words, **median ≈ 190**. 2020 ones are shorter (100–234), 2022 ones are mostly 190–230.
8. The arc: problem or hook (1 sentence) → gap or limitation of current practice → "we propose / introduce / provide X" → how (1–2 sentences) → **headline numbers** → implication or caveat. 11/13 give at least one concrete number (175B; 1.3B vs 175B, 100× fewer params; 67.5% MMLU; ~20% test cases; 5.85B pairs).
9. Footnotes or URLs inside the abstract are acceptable (4/13: Stiennon ×2, Side Effects URL, Precipice library, LAION project page). An abstract citation `[22]` appears in Side Effects.

### D. Introduction
10. A figure by page 2 in 12/13 (only Side Effects waited until p3). A page-1 figure in 2/13 (DT, CoT), placed below the abstract, full width or about 0.6\textwidth. The page-2 figure is a **hero result plot** in 7 cases (GPT-3, Stiennon, Precipice, InstructGPT, Chinchilla, CoT Fig. 2, LAION) or a **concept or example illustration** in 6 (Markov, DT, APPS, CodeRL, Reward Hacking, CoT Fig. 1).
11. An explicit contributions or findings block in 8/13:
    - bold numbered run-ins: Stiennon "(1)…(4)", Markov "1. 2. 3."
    - bold claim sentences: InstructGPT, 7 findings
    - a "**Contributions.**" paragraph: Side Effects
    - numbered lists: CoT, APPS difficulty levels
    - a recommendations table: Precipice Table 1
    - The rest state contributions in prose.
12. The research question is often stated in *italics* (Chinchilla, Reward Hacking). Real-world anecdotes are a common hook for specification gaming (Side Effects, Reward Hacking, Stiennon's ROUGE-as-proxy).

### E. Section order and names
13. Canonical order: Introduction → Related Work → Background / Preliminaries / Formalism → Method → Experiments / Results → Discussion (with Limitations) → Conclusion → unnumbered Acknowledgments → References → Checklist (2021+). Related Work is §2 in 8/13 and late in 4/13 (GPT-3 §6, DT §6, CoT §7, Reward Hacking §3 after an example section).
14. Headings use sentence case ("Related work", in Stiennon, DT and InstructGPT) or Title Case (others); both are fine if consistent. Question-form subsection or paragraph titles appear in 3/13 (DT §5.1–5.5, Stiennon §4.3 run-ins, Precipice §6 quote). "Discussion & Conclusion" is merged in 1/13 (Chinchilla).
15. Run-in bold paragraph headers (`\paragraph{Title.}`) appear in **13/13** and are the main structuring device below subsections. In results sections they are often the full claim ("**InstructGPT models show improvements in truthfulness over GPT-3.**").

### F. Limitations, broader impact, checklist (how conventions changed)
16. **2020:** a Broader Impact section is mandatory (3/3). Its form varies: a 6-page unnumbered section (GPT-3), a 2-paragraph unnumbered section after Discussion (Side Effects), or a run-in "**Broader impacts.**" paragraph inside Discussion (Stiennon). The "Acknowledgments and Disclosure of Funding" heading is new (Side Effects, Markov, Chinchilla use it; GPT-3 has "Funding Disclosures").
17. **2021:** the Broader Impact section is dropped and the **Paper Checklist** arrives, placed after References (Precipice p17) or in the supplement (Markov, DT). Societal impact shrinks to an unnumbered "Societal Impacts" section (Precipice) or a run-in "**Societal impact.**" plus "**Limitations.**" in the Conclusion (DT). The 2021 D&B paper (APPS) has neither.
18. **2022:** limitations become an explicit (sub)section in 4/6:
    - InstructGPT §5.2 Limitations and §5.3 Broader impacts
    - Reward Hacking §6.1 Limitations
    - CodeRL §5 Limitations and Broader Impacts
    - LAION §6 Technical Limitations and §7 Safety and Ethical Discussion
    - CoT and Chinchilla put limitations in Discussion paragraphs.
    - The checklist is in the PDF in 3/6 (InstructGPT before the references; Chinchilla and CodeRL after). Answers are rendered **[Yes] blue / [No] orange / [N/A] grey** with a one-line justification or section pointer.
19. Overall, limitations are acknowledged in 12/13 main texts; APPS is the exception.

### G. Figures
20. Figures per paper (main text): median 5 (range 1–12). Figure types:
    - line plots against scale or steps: 9/13
    - drawn pipeline or concept diagrams: 9/13 (Stiennon, InstructGPT, CodeRL ×3, LAION, DT, CoT, Markov, APPS, Reward Hacking)
    - bar charts: 6/13
    - heatmaps: 1/13
    - TikZ-style math diagrams: 2/13 (Markov MDPs, Reward Hacking polytopes)
21. Plot tooling: 7/13 embed **DejaVu Sans**, i.e. default matplotlib fonts were left on (Side Effects, DT, Markov, Precipice, APPS, Chinchilla, LAION). Others use Helvetica or Arial (Stiennon, InstructGPT, CodeRL) or **Times to match the body** (CoT). Sans-serif plot text against a serif body is the norm.
22. Palettes seen:
    - seaborn colorblind `#0173B2 #029E73 #DE8F05` (DT)
    - matplotlib tab10 `#1F77B4 #FF7F0E #2CA02C` (Chinchilla, Precipice-like)
    - the OpenAI pastel house set `#6CC0CE #8EC5F4 #72B585 #F2AE5B #C9563F` (Stiennon, InstructGPT)
    - black vs blue lines (CoT)
    - saturated primaries (APPS, LAION; the least polished look)
    - sequential colormaps (viridis / magma) for a continuous variable such as compute budget (Chinchilla); Blues for magnitude heatmaps and a red–green diverging map (Side Effects).
23. Shared conventions:
    - a **dashed black line for the reference or prior-best** (Stiennon, Chinchilla, CoT in orange dashed, Side Effects dotted vertical): 5/13
    - **the same colour per method across all figures** (Stiennon, InstructGPT, Side Effects, Precipice)
    - star markers to highlight named models (Chinchilla)
    - open-circle markers for the proposed method (CoT)
24. Legends are inside the panel or on top as a horizontal row (DT); outside on the right (Chinchilla, InstructGPT); or **replaced by direct coloured labels** (Stiennon Fig. 1; the cleanest hero plot of the set).
25. Grids are none or very light. No top/right spines in the polished ones (Stiennon, InstructGPT, DT). Line width is about 1.5–2 pt for main curves. Error ticks are thin.
26. Multi-panel figures label panels "(a)", "(b)" under each panel via subcaption (Markov, Side Effects, Stiennon, Reward Hacking, CodeRL tables), or with in-caption "**Left.** … **Right.** …" (Precipice, DT, Chinchilla "(left) / (center) / (right)"). Facet titles go in boxes above panels (InstructGPT) or as plot titles (APPS).
27. Counter-example: InstructGPT Fig. 4 has tick and label text of about 4 pt once scaled. Keep plot text ≥ 7 pt after scaling, i.e. set the matplotlib figure width to the real column width (5.5 in, or less for wrapfigures).

### H. Tables
28. 10/13 have main-text tables; InstructGPT, CoT and Reward Hacking have none. Rules: booktabs-style horizontal rules only in 7/10 (GPT-3, Stiennon, Markov, DT, Precipice, Chinchilla, LAION). Vertical rules in 3/10 (CodeRL fully ruled; Side Effects and APPS Table 2 use one vertical divider).
29. Best result in **bold** in at least 4/10 (GPT-3, DT, Chinchilla, CodeRL). "(Ours)" or "Ours" labels in headers or rows (DT, LAION). The own method is in the last row below a midrule (Chinchilla, CodeRL).
30. Uncertainty in tables: "mean ± std" with the seed count in the caption (DT, "across 3 seeds"); "(lo, hi)" intervals (Chinchilla); coloured ± deltas versus a baseline as superscripts (LAION). The remaining 7/10 table-bearing papers show **no** uncertainty in tables.
31. **No ↑/↓ arrows** in any 2020–2022 main-text table header (0/10). Direction goes in the caption ("Lower score is better.", "Higher …"). Arrows are a later (2023+) habit.
32. Caption position: **below in 7/10** (GPT-3, Stiennon, Side Effects, Markov, DT, APPS, LAION) and **above in 3/10** (Precipice, Chinchilla, CodeRL). The template's own instruction is captions above tables. Pick one and be consistent; "above" is safest.
33. Table font: `\small` to `\scriptsize` in dense tables (CodeRL, DT, APPS). Text and bullets inside cells are fine (Precipice Table 1). Comparison tables with coloured ✓/✗ are typical for dataset or benchmark papers (APPS).

### I. Captions
34. The label is always "Figure N:" / "Table N:" (GPT-3 used section numbering, "Figure 1.1"). Captions are 1–5 sentences, 2–3 typical.
35. A **bold lead phrase** opens the caption in 5/13 (GPT-3 bold first sentence; Chinchilla, CodeRL, Precipice, LAION). The others are plain. A good caption states (i) what is plotted, (ii) what error bars mean, and (iii) the takeaway (Chinchilla ends with a bold-italic takeaway; DT "… performs comparably or better").

### J. Uncertainty and evaluation reporting
36. 7/13 report variability on the key plots or tables:
    - Stiennon: 1 SE, footnoted once for the whole paper
    - Side Effects: ±1 std shaded
    - Markov: 95% CI shaded
    - Precipice: 95% stratified bootstrap
    - DT: ± over 3 seeds
    - InstructGPT: 95% CI
    - Chinchilla: bootstrap percentile intervals
    - APPS, CoT, CodeRL and LAION report point estimates only.
    - **State the interval type once, explicitly.**
37. Precipice (an Outstanding Paper) explicitly recommends interval estimates, IQM instead of mean or median, performance profiles, and probability of improvement when there are only a few runs. This is directly applicable to a small number of RL seeds.

### K. Math, theorems, algorithms
38. Numbered display equations: median about 3. Theory papers use 0–3 numbered but many unnumbered; CodeRL uses 10. Notation:
    - calligraphic sets: MDP = (S, A, T, R, γ) or (S, A, P, R)
    - E with the distribution as subscript
    - θ for policy params, φ for the critic, π^SFT / π^RL superscripts
    - hats for estimates (L̂, q̂)
    - **R̃ (tilde) for the proxy reward vs R for the true reward** (Reward Hacking)
    - piecewise rewards typeset with `cases` (CodeRL)
    - the KL-penalised reward displayed once (Stiennon, InstructGPT).
39. Theorem environments in 3/13 (Side Effects, Markov, Reward Hacking): bold "Definition 1." label with an italic statement, numbered globally (Reward Hacking) or within section (Markov 3.1, 4.1). Proofs always go to the appendix ("All proofs are presented in Appendix B").
40. Algorithm boxes in 2/13: algorithmic style with line numbers and ▷ comments (Markov), or Python-like pseudocode with coloured comments (DT).

### L. Citations, links, colour in text
41. Citation style:
    - numeric `[n]` in 7/13
    - alpha `[RWC+19]` in 1/13
    - author-year natbib in 5/13
    - The shift: 2020 has 0/3 author-year, 2021 has 1/4 (APPS), **2022 has 4/6** (InstructGPT, CoT, Reward Hacking, CodeRL).
42. Number of references: median ≈ 66 (range 24–116; about 95+ for GPT-3). A 4-page paper can do with about 15–30.
43. Link colouring:
    - blue colorlinks text: GPT-3, Markov, Chinchilla, CoT
    - magenta: Precipice
    - black / no colour: Stiennon, APPS
    - **default hyperref boxes (green / red / cyan rectangles): 5/13** (DT, InstructGPT, Reward Hacking, CodeRL, LAION). They are visible in every on-screen render and look unfinished. Use `\hypersetup{colorlinks=true, citecolor=..., linkcolor=..., urlcolor=...}` or `hidelinks`.
44. Semantic colour in body text is rare and purposeful: Precipice's magenta for recommended tools, LAION's green/red deltas, APPS's ✓/✗, the checklist answer colours. No paper colours whole sentences.
45. Typography conventions:
    - *italics* for model names (Chinchilla, Gopher) and defined terms
    - **bold** for terms at first definition (Reward Hacking, Precipice)
    - small caps for acronyms and coined names (Side Effects AUP, Markov TASKQ, Precipice DER)
    - `typewriter` for env, dataset and function names and URLs.
46. Footnotes in 13/13: equal contribution (fn 1), code or data URLs, implementation caveats, and a once-per-paper convention ("error bars represent 1 SE"). Footnotes inside the abstract are allowed.

### M. Appendix and supplement
47. In 2020–2022 the proceedings PDF usually stops after References (and the checklist). The appendix is a separate supplement, referenced as "Appendix C.6" (11/13). GPT-3 is the exception, bundling it. If the appendix is separate, compile with real labels: CodeRL shipped "Appendix ??" three times.

### N. Space-saving tricks observed (counts)
48. The tricks:
    - **wrapfigure** for small plots or tables: 6/13 (Stiennon, Precipice ×4, APPS, CoT ×4, Reward Hacking, CodeRL table)
    - **side-by-side minipages** (figure + figure or figure + table): 5/13 (Stiennon, APPS, LAION, Precipice, CodeRL)
    - run-in `\paragraph` instead of `\subsection`: 13/13
    - merging Discussion and Conclusion, or putting Limitations as a run-in inside the Conclusion (Chinchilla, DT, Stiennon, CoT)
    - URLs moved to footnotes (LAION 12+)
    - subtables (a)/(b) under one caption (CodeRL)
    - small-multiple grids sharing one axis label (CoT, Markov, Precipice)
    - `\small` captions (Precipice only)
    - inline lists via run-in numbers "(1) … (2) …" instead of itemize (Markov abstract, Stiennon contributions)
    - Avoid CodeRL-style negative `\vspace` that makes rules collide with text.

### O. Topic-specific takeaways for our reward-hacking / text-to-SQL paper
49. These papers frame the core motif the same way: **a proxy (ROUGE, a reward model, unit tests) diverges from the true objective under optimisation pressure.**
    - Stiennon Fig. 5: RM score keeps rising while human preference falls, plotted against KL.
    - Reward Hacking: a formal definition, with a red-proxy vs blue-true Goodhart curve.
    - APPS: test-case false positives.
    - CodeRL: graded execution reward −1 / −0.6 / −0.3 / +1.
    - A "proxy vs true metric vs optimisation steps" plot is the recognisable genre figure. Use 2 colours (proxy and true), a dashed reference line, and a shaded CI.
50. Directly reusable phrasing and notation:
    - "proxy reward R̃ vs true reward R"
    - "unhackable"
    - "over-optimization"
    - "alignment tax" (InstructGPT)
    - "strict accuracy vs test-case average" (APPS)
    - "verifier" (CoT abstract)
    - CodeRL already cites text-to-SQL RL (Zhong et al. 2018, Seq2SQL; Xu et al. 2018).

## Part 3: The 15 most important rules (for the 4-page poster paper)
1. **Keep the template untouched:** 10 pt Times, 5.5 in text block, 10 pt captions (12/13). Only change the footer line to the symposium's venue text.
2. **Title:** Title Case, 5–8 words, either a claim ("X Elicits Y") or "Name: what it does". Bold and centred between the template rules.
3. **Abstract:** one paragraph of 150–200 words. Problem → gap → "we propose" → how → **one or two concrete numbers** → implication or caveat. 11/13 include headline numbers.
4. **Figure on page 1 or the top of page 2:** a pipeline or teaser illustration (6/13) or a hero result plot (7/13). For 4 pages, put it at the top of p1 below the abstract (DT, CoT), or as a wrapfigure.
5. **End the intro with explicit contributions:** bold numbered run-ins or bold claim sentences (8/13).
6. **Structure below sections with bold run-in `\paragraph{Claim.}` headers** (13/13). In Results, make each header the finding itself (InstructGPT).
7. **Tables use booktabs horizontal rules only** (7/10), with the best value in bold, "(Ours)" labelled, and the own method last below a midrule. No vertical rules or `\scriptsize` crowding. Use no ↑/↓ arrows (0/10 in this era); say "higher is better" in the caption.
8. **Pick one table-caption position and keep it:** "above" per the template (the corpus split 7 below / 3 above). Figure captions always go below.
9. **Captions:** a bold lead phrase, then what is shown, what the error bars are, and a one-sentence takeaway (Chinchilla, Precipice, CodeRL, LAION, GPT-3).
10. **Always show uncertainty and name it once** (SE, std over k seeds, or 95% bootstrap CI; 7/13 do). With few RL runs, follow Precipice: IQM plus stratified-bootstrap CIs or probability of improvement.
11. **Plots:**
    - sans-serif text (or Times to match the body), sized for a 5.5 in or smaller width, so ticks stay ≥ 7 pt
    - a colourblind-safe categorical palette (seaborn colorblind `#0173B2 #029E73 #DE8F05` or tab10)
    - **one fixed colour per method across every figure**
    - a dashed black line for the reference or baseline
    - direct labels instead of legends where possible
    - light or no grid; no top/right spines.
12. **Show the genre figure:** proxy reward vs true (execution-verified) accuracy over training, i.e. the Goodhart / over-optimisation curve (Stiennon Fig. 5, Reward Hacking Fig. 2). Define the proxy as R̃ vs the true R, and write the verifier reward as a `cases` equation (CodeRL).
13. **Include Limitations and broader impact even in 4 pages.** 12/13 do, and from 2021 the checklist asks for it. A run-in "**Limitations.**" plus "**Broader impact.**" paragraph pair inside the Discussion or Conclusion is the compact form (Stiennon, DT).
14. **Citations:** natbib author-year (the 2022 majority, 4/6) or numeric. Set `colorlinks` (blue) or `hidelinks`. **Never ship default hyperref boxes** (5/13 did) and **never ship "??"** (CodeRL).
15. **Save space with these devices:** wrapfigure (6/13), side-by-side minipages (5/13), a merged Discussion & Conclusion, URLs in footnotes, (a)/(b) subfigures sharing one caption. Don't shrink fonts or margins, and don't use negative-vspace hacks.
